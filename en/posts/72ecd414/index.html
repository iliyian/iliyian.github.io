Roosting crows cry out, breaking the traces of scattered blossoms;
Beneath the bright moon, my soul breaks in vain.

Startled by the urgent rain, resenting the tangled wind;
Fearing the advancing frost, I bar the door within.
Braving the cold, withered leaves harbor a heavy, sinking grief;
After the rain, my sorrow grows deeper beyond relief.

Joy of the Moving Oriole

Wave-shadows sway, leaves drift in yellow flight;
The wind rolls up and hides the moonlight.
In the evening woods, the golden well surges with starlight’s gleam;
Looking back, the mist is vast as a dream.

Soul-melting wine, a rosy face grown thin;
Standing alone, pure wind fills my sleeves within.
My fragrant heart has grown cold, the night is faintly chill;
Before the broken mirror, I bid farewell to my crimson makeup still.

Partridge Sky

Ten leagues of spring wind roll up the blinds for me; seal-script incense burns my dreams as I stand on the morning veranda free.
In the small courtyard of chiming bells, orioles warble in the rain; in the tiered tower of locked regrets, the phoenix brushes the zither’s strain.
Aloeswood smoke curls, the jade goblet stands idle; by the vermilion terrace and jasper mirror, my prime years bridle.
The lamp-flower burns out as the crescent moon ascends; afar I watch the Three Stars circling heaven’s nine bends.

Like a Dream

Heartbroken by the cuckoo’s urgent cry;
Beside the goblet, moths fly and candle-flames dance high.
Chill, chill, a single silver hook above
Shines in vain upon the towers and the ferry-cove.
Listless, listless;
Overnight, whence has the frost sprung forth?

Untitled

One pities the autumn clouds that drift away; the sun sets, yet the evening breeze doth stay.
Only the wrinkled shadows of the pool are seen; unending is my longing, deep and keen.
High the mountains, evening clouds in autumn’s hold; I breathe the sweet fragrance of osmanthus gold.
In the mountain temple, a zither sounds its strain; note after note stirs the drooping willows’ train.
Turbid wine consoles the traveler’s woe; raising my cup, the wind fills my sleeves aglow.

Untitled

Through the long night, the moon is cold and the wind is still; the cuckoo’s cry breaks through, waking me from my dream’s chill.
The curtains are rolled up, pale clouds drift light; in the courtyard, remnant blossoms cast tangled shadows in the night.

A pair of swallows bring fragrant mud along the rainy lane; over a single cup of clear tea, I recall and reflect in vain.
Incense curls like mist, the mat climbs with ice; the jade goblet intoxicates the moon as my eyes first close thrice.

Rouging the Lips

Deep in the twelfth watch of the night,
In the small courtyard of cherry blossoms, one stands long in the light.
The pure breeze wishes to dance;
In an instant, countless petals fly by chance.

The sinking moon is deep in clouds;
I only ask, where art thou in the shrouds?
The cherry blossom tree—
Before the tree, the blossoms fade;
Beneath the tree, one stands long in the shade.

Rouging the Lips

The setting sun and a migrating wild goose;
Clear frost, evening rain, and grass upon the sandy truce.
The frigid river stretches vast and wide;
Leaves scatter down the autumn wind’s wayside.

Ascending the high tower once more,
The moon is waning, the starlight poor.
The jasper cup is overturned;
Longing remains unfulfilled and burned;
In the mirror, a rosy face has aged and mourned.

Seven-Character Regulated Verse

Secret affection and secluded joy—how could they be lightly betrayed? In the rain, a young gallant wanders, withered and frayed.
Myriad stars shine chill, my feelings are hard to tell; the solitary moon lingers fondly, yet the dream does not dwell.
Outside the window, a lean poplar is entwined by the wind’s grace; at midnight, in the cold rain, a traveler paces in place.
Upon the plantain leaves, drop after drop rings clear; gazing up at the azure sky, several lines of tears appear.

Xingdong’s Seven-Character Regulated Verse

In the vermilion tower, behind embroidered blinds, seal-script incense burns low; full of passion, I dare not forget my lord Xiao.
Emerald screens, silver hooks, and painted candles cold; jade mats, golden tripods, and the night rain’s long hold.
Reed-pipes and flutes are speechless, the zither-strings sound bleak; by the Xiao and湘 (Xiang) rivers, there is regret as wild geese wander weak.
Peach-blossom stationery bears my dream in a single letter’s space; pouring out my whole heart, I write in lines of tears’ trace.

Joy of Returning to Court

Churning waves and rolling clouds dim the twilight hue.
Rustling, the cold wind blows as wild geese cry anew.
Along the road, the autumn grass is desolate and bare; where horse-hooves have trodden, no trace remains there.
A wandering traveler from the Three Chu lands;
Thin smoke faintly veils a deserted village post-stand.
Resenting the rustling sound—wind and rain piercing the window-pane, all dripping straight into my heart’s domain.

The frosty moon hangs low, full of tender grace.
In the jade pavilion, by the latticed window, one is lost in memory’s embrace.
Cicadas chirp round the stone steps, never finding peace; beyond the low wall and overgrown path, my gaze finds no release.
In the vault of heaven, ten thousand grains of stars gleam;
Pale clouds come and go, cruelly parting the stream.
Tears follow the wind; standing alone on the mountain crest, a single note rises from a Qiang flute’s breast.

Five-Character Regulated Verse

Moonlight enters the long corridor’s space, filling it bright with white light’s grace.
Footsteps echo in solitary sound; light leaves are left to fly unbound.
My sleeves rise with the wind’s caress; my heart forgets the world’s distress.
Doth the Moon Goddess drink wine tonight? Clear water falls upon my robes so white.

Spring in Wuling

In the courtyard, plantains tint the little path with green; bamboo shoots emerge before the steps’ scene.
A pair of swallows twitter, nesting 'neath the eaves;
Light rain leaps upon the swing’s leaves.

A boat enters the clear-water cove of lotus blooms, gradually waking ripples from their glooms.
A gentle breeze blows the willows, catkins fall upon my shoulder;
In a sea of flowers, butterflies flutter bolder.

Treading on Sedge

Mountains screen green mountains, bordering the lake’s green grass.
A magnolia boat with light oars is ringed by tiny waves that pass.
Among the woods, a traveler of Chu is drunk on blossoms fair; in the tower, pale hands thread the needle with skillful care.

Threads of remnant clouds, the bright moon shines white and clear.
In the endless cold night, the sound of the wind grows still and near.
Tossing beneath the red waves of the quilt, I cannot find sleep; out of nowhere tears arise, for love is too hard to speak or keep.

Immortals at the Magpie Bridge

A single pure song, a whole night of bright moon; on a rattan bed behind paper curtains, sleep comes not soon.
Following the wind, delicate willows sway on their own; where ten thousand lanterns shine, Chang’an cannot bear the groan.
Shooting stars dot the sky, slender clouds drift in threads; my heart is in turmoil, and the clear tea is hard to sip in its stead.
In the river’s heart, the fishing fires seem about to sleep; heavy is the jade hairpin—how can I pillow this spring sorrow deep?

Immortals at the Magpie Bridge

Among the clouds, the stars scatter; in the river’s heart, the moon melts away; how many blossoms has the courtyard lost today?
Down the secluded, deep path I wish to seek; yet I fear there is nowhere for my steps to speak.
New wine in a golden goblet, green dregs floating light; behind the curtain, a faint chill and the rain of night.
Ten thousand sorrowful thoughts are shaken again by the wind; breathing the dragon-brain incense, its fragrance follows where my dreams haveinned.

Immortals at the Magpie Bridge

The swing sways to and fro, early birds sing of spring; dots of delicate blossoms hide where the willows cling.
Outside the city, fragrant grasses are greener than the sky; sweat dampens my華衣 (splendid robes) and blue sleeves high.
A Penglai fairyland, heavenly brocade and kingfisher-green, surpassed not even by distant peaks emerging from the clouds’ sheen.
Several gusts of the long wind indulge their wildness free, only blowing wrinkles across the river’s heart to see.

Immortals at the Magpie Bridge

Gusts of pure breeze, resplendent moonlight’s grace; in the dimness, I pour fine wine alone in this place.
Gazing to the ends of the earth, the road is endless and vast; the house is deep and quiet, my heart in dejection cast.
At the Magpie Bridge they have met, the magpies’ cries have dispersed; though magpie feathers fill the street, who cares for the cursed?
Water is clear, moistening jade—jade both square and round; the night is solitary and cold, my ghostly soul returns to yesterday’s ground.

Sixteen-Character Lyric

Home—
One gaze across the misty streams where evening rains roam.
Brushing past flying geese, the moon’s reflection rocks on the autumn river’s foam.

Untitled

Early birds call out note by note, summoning my dreams back home; o’er green hills, the morning sun sprinkles spring light’s foam.
Thick green leaves conceal the gauzy mist; along the endless ancient steps, crape myrtles twist.
The east wind understands not the blossoms’ heart; with pale hands I lightly pluck them, treading duckweed as I depart.

Remembering the Qin Maiden

The sound of rain falls on and on;
In the small shop across the street, a solitary lamp shines wan.
A solitary lamp shines wan;
With bowed head I wait in vain, while all others have already gone.

The moon rises, the wind sobs, about to set forth into the dark;
In the cold pond, no stars at the sky’s edge leave a mark.
Stars at the sky’s edge leave no mark;
Promised, yet thou comest not—the rain falls with a ceaseless spark.

Joy of Meeting

Carrying spring, a fine rain falls in the courtyard’s space,
Lush and green its grace.
In the emerald trees, how many orioles warble, sharing the spring wind’s embrace?

Spring’s splendor is heavy, spring’s colors tease, and my mood melts away;
Strolling idly down the flower-path beneath the willows’ shade, we meet again today.

Remembering the Qin Maiden

Passing deep into the wood,
Tender buds of fresh green hear a sparse sound where they stood.
Hear a sparse sound where they stood;
Brushing aside light smoke, through bamboo crevices a breeze is brewed.

Year after year, when the snow is spent, new roots spring forth;
In garden after garden, flower stamens renew their fragrant worth.
Renew their fragrant worth;
How much pent-up sorrow is there? Let it all be consigned to the golden goblet’s girth.

Untitled

The moonlight takes up the pen of a shooting star
To write whispers of love to the myriad stars afar.

In the autumn wind of the courtyard,
Fallen leaves recount the tale of parting hard.

Twilight drapes itself in splendid, iridescent clouds;
Whither flies the lost bird through the shrouds?

Night after night, I chant your book of verse,
Fantasizing news of your return to rehearse.

Sands of the Washing Stream

Flying swallows, light smoke, and willows of azure-green;
Behind the clouds in the vast sky, moonlight pours its sheen.
Reed-pipes and flutes are like water, the night like ice keen.

Beating a fan with flowing tassels, the wind turns gentle and mild;
In the courtyard of fallen blossoms, the rain is clear and undefiled.
A jasper cup of emerald wine reflects a sky with stars piled.

Gazing at Jiangnan

O stars of heaven, shining upon my mat’s light chill!
Ten thousand threads of tender grief are too much to bear; a thousand bitter longings climb the balustrade still.
When the lamp dies, it is harder yet to endure the thrill.

Gazing at Jiangnan

Soft, rosy hands tease and stir my heart’s core.
Cold snow beats against the window, keeping me from sleep; a lone star circles the heavenly hook all night o’er.
Tears fall, harder yet to gather than before.

Gazing at Jiangnan

A goblet of crystal glass cannot bear the evening wind’s sway.
Evening rain, clear frost, and the moon at the river’s floor; a skiff leaves the bank in the driving rain’s fray.
Outside the pavilion stands a cold, solitary bridge of gray.

Gazing at Jiangnan

Wild geese on the sandy shoal break through the smoke in the evening light’s glow.
Reeds rustle mournfully as the wind sighs; by the long pavilion, the cold moon lingers slow.
With knitted brows, I recall thy dark tresses of long ago.

Gazing at Jiangnan

After twilight falls, the moon’s shadow grows more desolate still.
Over turbid wine, to whom can I pour out my bitter breast? The clear, cold night enters the casement sill.
All o’er the ground, cold frost piles its chill.

Palindrome Poem

The frigid river mirrors the moon in the cold wind’s swift flight; the quiet courtyard congeals with frost where autumn leaves fade.
Where faded leaves and autumn frost congeal, the courtyard is quiet; swift wind and cold moon mirror the river’s frigid shade.

Untitled

Within the walls of Chang’an, from whose upper chamber flies the music of a jade flute?
Past all the bustling clamor, carefree spring blossoms and emerald willows sway mute.
Migrating wild geese cry with urgency; a letter is hard to entrust, and one weeps alone.
The night is quiet and still; rolling up the blind, the cold wind embraces my traveler’s gown.

Fisherman’s Song

Twirling the ruined splendor of spring within my hand,
Who is it that stands wan and haggard in the east wind’s land?
The bright moon is cold, the rain falls misty and grand;
Dripping upon my heart, my sorrow grows harder to withstand.

Two Modern-Vernacular Regulated Verses

If I should become a little bird, I would seek out the crimson of the flame;
With my heart’s blood I would scorch the icy wilds, then swiftly die amidst the hell of calamity’s frame.

If I should become a green leaf, I would drive away that flying insect pest;
With my broad garments of green, I would shield against the gale in the sky’s crest.

Sands of the Washing Stream

A single tune of Yangguan holds ten thousand threads of grace;
A frosty sky steeps the moon as a shooting star skims space;
Though fleeting light scatters completely, my heart finds no peaceful place.

Night’s hue descends upon me, and in sorrow alone I lean.

The Seventh Night

In the idle courtyard, blossoms thrive by the pond-side willow tree; tonight, the myriad stars are naturally different to see.
The bright moon roams alone within the Silver Sea; twin shining stars meet upon the Magpie Bridge free.
Fluttering flying sleeves are lifted by the pure breeze; trailing rainbow skirts stain tearful cheeks with crimson decrees.
May lovers in the mortal realm endure forevermore; high in the tower we drink together till dawn invades the eastern shore.

Pride of the Fisherman

Boundless the sleet and snow, fierce the wind’s might; the winter plum suffers not from the cold winter’s blight.
Its stamens bloom with secluded fragrance, still charming and bright;
Deep within the woods, where a hundred flowers have perished, one may still reach its height.

The pale moon lingers fondly as frost melts on the ground; in the idle courtyard, swallows call that spring is homeward bound.
Fallen petals of red jade blossoms are like tears profound;
Though its body dies, riding the wind afar, its fragrance is left to the world around.

Untitled

A cold window without a moon.
Dim starlight, and a bitter cicada chirping with urgency.
I cannot bear to draw the pearl curtains close, only fearing that on the morrow, the gentle light will be drowned.
The candle flame has just gone out, and a chill, fierce wind scrapes past my side.
Secret resentment surges, my eyes can scarce endure it—how can I bear these tears pouring forth in a torrent!

Wounded in form, every word is written in fresh blood.
Having lost a dear friend, I know well who is the lesser!
Why do I sigh long without a word? Where there are tears, could it ever be joy?
Through the long, endless night, only the warm candle before me speaks.
The wooden desk is cold, and my heart is equally chill; the brush is withered, and the lamp-oil has just run dry.
